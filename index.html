About lafspline

lafspline looks for the least ambitious curve the data allow: the simplest one, by a measure you choose, that stays inside a confidence region for the regression function f. It works in two phases, both with P-splines (B-splines on equally spaced knots with a penalty on the differences of their coefficients).

Phase 1: the region

  • Bootstrap band (the default): a simultaneous band around a P-spline fit, from refits with random fractional weights on the observations, so it can be asymmetric and wider where the noise is. It covers the smoothed f, f as the same penalty would smooth it.
  • P-spline band: a simultaneous band from the P-spline's Bayesian covariance, with its critical value by Monte Carlo. It covers f itself if f is smooth.
  • Aₙ (Davies, Kovac & Meise): bounds on the sums of the residuals over intervals of the observations, with τ calibrated by Monte Carlo for the coverage. It assumes nothing about the smoothness of f.

The bands' λ is chosen by GCV and then divided by 4 (undersmoothing), so that the band follows bends closely enough to cover them; AIC, two rules of thumb and a given λ are there for comparison. Each region states what it covers, with what probability and under which assumptions; the coverage figures are from simulations, and the readouts say which apply.

Phase 2: the fit

A second P-spline is penalized severely for leaving the region, slightly for its distance from the region's center, and for ambition: how far it is from a straight line (or a constant). The ambition knob is in decades from a*, the largest ambition before the fit leaves the region, so 0 is the simplest fit inside on any data, lower values follow more detail, and higher ones leave the region. Ambition can be measured as the squared differences (smooth everywhere), the number of bends or steps (straight or flat pieces), the number of bends with rounding and costs for extremes and inflections, or the differences' sizes to a power q.

Every fit says which region it was checked against, whether it stays inside, and by how much it leaves where it does. Approximate methods (iterated weights, the adaptive ridge, majorize-minimize) say so, and report whether they converged. The readouts also say whether a constant or a straight line would fit inside the region.

Using the page

  • Choose an example, a simulated signal, or paste or drop CSV data. Hover the chart for exact values, and the ⓘ icons for what each setting and readout means.
  • The address bar holds the current settings, so reloading keeps them and Copy link shares them. Pasted data is included when it compresses to a short enough link.
  • Export saves the data and fits as CSV, at the observations or on the check grid, and the chart as SVG, PNG or JPEG, as drawn (hidden layers are left out of the image, not the tables).
  • The example data sets are third-party measurements, included for demonstration with their sources cited (shown with each data set). They are not covered by lafspline's license.
