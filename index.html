About lafspline

lafspline looks for the least ambitious curve the data allow: the simplest one, by a measure you choose, that stays inside a confidence region for the regression function f. It works in two phases, both with P-splines (B-splines on equally spaced knots with a penalty on the differences of their coefficients).

Phase 1: the region

  • Bootstrap band (the default): a simultaneous band around a P-spline fit, from refits with random fractional weights on the observations, so it can be asymmetric and wider where the noise is. It covers the smoothed f, f as the same penalty would smooth it.
  • P-spline band: a simultaneous band from the P-spline's Bayesian covariance, with its critical value by Monte Carlo. It covers f itself if f is smooth.
  • Aₙ (Davies, Kovac & Meise): bounds on the sums of the residuals over intervals of the observations, with τ calibrated by Monte Carlo for the coverage. It assumes nothing about the smoothness of f, but more than the bands about the noise: skewed or uneven noise lowers its coverage far more. By default it checks only the intervals spanning at least 5% of the x range, so it answers to features at least that wide.

The bands' λ comes from a feature scale: the shortest features of interest, as a share of the x range (10% by default, raised for small data sets so that a feature spans at least 10 observations). λ is set so that a sine of that period keeps half its amplitude, so the same scale means the same on every data set. GCV (divided by 4, undersmoothing), AIC, two rules of thumb and a given λ are the alternatives. Each region states what it covers, with what probability and under which assumptions; the coverage figures are from simulations, and the readouts say which apply.

Phase 2: the fit

A second P-spline is penalized severely for leaving the region, slightly for its distance from the region's center, and for ambition: how far it is from a straight line (or a constant). The ambition knob is in decades from a*, the largest ambition before the fit leaves the region, so 0 is the simplest fit inside on any data, lower values follow more detail, and higher ones leave the region. Ambition can be measured as the squared differences (smooth everywhere), the number of bends or steps (straight or flat pieces), shaped bends (that number, plus rounding and costs for extremes and inflections), or the differences' sizes to a power q.

Every fit says which region it was checked against, whether it stays inside, and by how much it leaves where it does. Approximate methods (iterated weights, the adaptive ridge, majorize-minimize) say so, and report whether they converged. The readouts also say whether a constant or a straight line would fit inside the region.

Using the page

  • Choose an example, a simulated signal, or paste or drop CSV data. Hover the chart for exact values, and the ⓘ icons for what each setting and readout means.
  • Gallery shows several data sets at once with the same settings, each chosen above its chart; a pane's ↗ opens it in the single view with its readouts.
  • The ↺ ↶ ↷ buttons above the settings reset them to their defaults, undo and redo (⌘Z and ⇧⌘Z, or Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Shift+Z); they leave the data sets as they are.
  • The address bar holds the current settings, so reloading keeps them and Copy link shares them. Pasted data is included when it compresses to a short enough link.
  • Export saves the data and fits as CSV, at the observations or on the check grid, and the chart as SVG, PNG or JPEG, as drawn (hidden layers are left out of the image, not the tables).
  • The example data sets are third-party measurements, included for demonstration with their sources cited (shown with each data set). They are not covered by lafspline's license.
